Bioleather

Bioleather is a sustainable alternative to traditional leather made from natural materials. I grew my own bioleather using a symbiotic culture of bacteria and yeast (SCOBY) and black tea (Yorkshire tea!). Bioleather also known as kombucha leather is a plant based and vegan alternative to leather. It is grown via fermentation and made of bacterial cellulose nanofibres. Once grown, it can be dried and oiled to yield a flexible and durable material that can be modelled and sewn.

The BIG SCOBY - 10 June 2026 I'm going to try grow a larger SCOBY as well using SCOBY v3 as a starter. Method I added 4 cups of strong tea (brewed with 4 tea bags and 40g of sugar) to a large flat box. I added 200ml of SCOBY v3 liquid and stirred. I added 3L of water to cool the tea. I have left covered for around a week. Observations: (10 June) orange/brown translucent liquid, a couple of bubbles and small SCOBYs on the surface.
My SCOBY v3 - 3 June 2026 Method I removed the SCOBY with a hole and added another 200ml of tea (containing 2 tea bags and 20g of sugar) Stir liquid in dish Observations: (10 June) orange brown liquid with opaque precipitate at the bottom.
My SCOBY v2 - 3 June 2026 Method After removing the previous SCOBY, I added 200ml of tea (containing 2 tea bags and 20g of sugar) Stir liquid in dish Leave flat dish on heating pad (~15C) and cover with cloth. Observations: (19 May) orange/brown translucent liquid, with a few bubbles on the surface. Some small floating SCOBYs from previous batch. (10 June) SCOBY formed on top but tin foil has created a hole.
My SCOBY — 18 May 2026 Method Dissolve 150g caster sugar in 440ml boiling water. Add 8 tea bags (YT), leave for 15 minutes, remove bags. Let cool. Add 1L water. Add 500ml ginger and turmeric kombucha. Stir and fill flat dish approximately halfway. Store remaining liquid in jars in the cold room. Leave flat dish on heating pad (~15C) and cover with cloth. Observations: (19 May) orange/brown translucent liquid, with a few bubbles on the surface. No visible growth yet.
(23 May) Liquid slightly lighter in colour. Light sediment gathering at the bottom of the dish. Topped up with ~100ml of tea. (31 May) Surface completely covered with ~2cm thick SCOBY. (2 June) SCOBY approximately 3cm thick. Removed from dish, cleaned with dish soap and dried. Left to try on wooden sticks on heating mat (~15C). (10 June) Completely dry and stiff. Orange translucent material, very light.
