Bioleather

Bioleather is a sustainable alternative to traditional leather made from natural materials. I grew my own bioleather using a symbiotic culture of bacteria and yeast (SCOBY) and black tea (Yorkshire tea!). Bioleather also known as kombucha leather is a plant based and vegan alternative to leather. It is grown via fermentation and made of bacterial cellulose nanofibres. Once grown, it can be dried and oiled to yield a flexible and durable material that can be modelled and sewn.

Different tea types - 19 June 2026 I have decided to experiment with a couple different teas available to me to see how this affects the SCOBY produced. I have started with 4 different teas; a mulled apple, a blackcurrant and blueberry to see how sugar content and hibiscus effects the SCOBY,a green tea to see how the colour is affected and lapsang souchong to see if the smokey flavour affects the smell of the SCOBY. Method I added 1 cup of each tea containing 10g of sugar to 100ml of water and 10ml of SCOBY v3 liquid. I have left the dishes covered for 5 days. (23 June) SCOBYs have formed but I have left them one more day to see if they thicken.

The BIG SCOBY - 10 June 2026 I'm going to try grow a larger SCOBY as well using SCOBY v3 as a starter. Method I added 4 cups of strong tea (brewed with 4 tea bags and 40g of sugar) to a large flat box. I added 200ml of SCOBY v3 liquid and stirred. I added 3L of water to cool the tea. I have left covered for around a week. Observations: (10 June) orange/brown translucent liquid, a couple of bubbles and small SCOBYs on the surface. (23 June) fully grown SCOBY, with an approximate 1cm thickness. I have removed it from the liquid and left it to dry for at least 3 days. I have also added 200ml more of strong sweet tea for the liquid to feed off.
My SCOBY v3 - 3 June 2026 Method I removed the SCOBY with a hole and added another 200ml of tea (containing 2 tea bags and 20g of sugar) Stir liquid in dish Observations: (10 June) orange brown liquid with opaque precipitate at the bottom.
My SCOBY v2 - 3 June 2026 Method After removing the previous SCOBY, I added 200ml of tea (containing 2 tea bags and 20g of sugar) Stir liquid in dish Leave flat dish on heating pad (~15C) and cover with cloth. Observations: (19 May) orange/brown translucent liquid, with a few bubbles on the surface. Some small floating SCOBYs from previous batch. (10 June) SCOBY formed on top but tin foil has created a hole.
My SCOBY — 18 May 2026 Method Dissolve 150g caster sugar in 440ml boiling water. Add 8 tea bags (YT), leave for 15 minutes, remove bags. Let cool. Add 1L water. Add 500ml ginger and turmeric kombucha. Stir and fill flat dish approximately halfway. Store remaining liquid in jars in the cold room. Leave flat dish on heating pad (~15C) and cover with cloth. Observations: (19 May) orange/brown translucent liquid, with a few bubbles on the surface. No visible growth yet. (23 May) Liquid slightly lighter in colour. Light sediment gathering at the bottom of the dish. Topped up with ~100ml of tea. (31 May) Surface completely covered with ~2cm thick SCOBY. (2 June) SCOBY approximately 3cm thick. Removed from dish, cleaned with dish soap and dried. Left to try on wooden sticks on heating mat (~15C). (10 June) Completely dry and stiff. Orange translucent material, very light.
