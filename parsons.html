Healthy & Sustainable Materials

Parsons School of Design · Healthy Materials Lab

I took the course when I first realised where I wanted to take the direction of my studies of materials, healthier and more sustainable materials, particularly for sustainable housing. The course is designed and taught by the Healthy Materials Lab at Parsons in New York. It contains input from toxicologists, environmental pediatricians, green chemists, architects, contractors and environmental justice researchers. It ran in two parts, first the studying the science behind the toxicity of materials, how they offgas, shed particles and erode and chemicals can enter the body through the air or water. The major case studies were lead and asbestos, in both evidence of harm was visible early but due to the usefulness of the materials was ignored and they became ubiquitous anyways.

The course also highlighted the inequality involved with who healthy living is available to. Redlining, poverty and proximity to industrial areas concentrate toxic exposure on specific communities. Regulatory systems meant to protect these communities largely don't, many chemicals in use since 1976 were grandfathered in under US law and never properly assessed. From a design angle, the life cycle framing was an interesting learning point. The use phase is often the smallest part of a material's impact, with extraction and disposal falling disproportionately on communities with the least power to impact where these processes take place.

The second part of the course was about how health is written into a brief maintained across specification, the entire construction team and lifecycle. It is about the timing, the decisions that matter the most are made earliest as you can't work these solutions back into the project at the end. The course finished on circularity and biomaterials, where it connects most directly with my own work.

I'm interested in the materials we build and live with, how they affect our health and how the built environment shapes the communities that use them. I have a particular focus on healthier and more sustainable materials for social housing and shared community spaces.
